# Sacratan Mechanicus — Overview

> **Canon note:** This file is the high-level canonical reference for the Mechanicus of Sacratus. Unmarked statements are established canon. Material explicitly marked **PROVISIONAL**, **OPEN**, or **UNKNOWN IN-UNIVERSE** retains that status.

## Identity

**Sacratus is a Forge World** and the industrial heart of the Sacratus system.

During the Great Crusade and Horus Heresy era, Sacratus is already an important **Mechanicum logistics and industrial nexus**. Its value lies not only in individual manufactoria but in the wider network of production, maintenance, storage, transport, and technical expertise concentrated around the Forge World and its associated system infrastructure.

After the Heresy, the local priesthood continues as part of the **Adeptus Mechanicus**. This directory uses *Mechanicus* for the faction across the setting while retaining *Mechanicum* where historically appropriate to the pre-Adeptus era.

The Sacratan Mechanicus remains loyal to Mars and the Imperium throughout the Long Vigil. Isolation does not turn it into a formally independent or separatist machine cult. The defining problem is instead how a Forge World built as part of a much larger Imperial industrial network survives for almost nineteen centuries when that wider network becomes unreachable.

## From nexus to closed industrial system

Before the Breaking, Sacratus can rely on flows that extend beyond the system: raw materials, specialist components, replacement personnel, external knowledge, shipping, and the wider redundancy of Imperial industry.

The Breaking removes that depth.

From that point onward, Sacratan industry must increasingly reproduce, repair, substitute, reclaim, and prioritize from within the resources still reachable inside the system.

The central Mechanicus problem of the Long Vigil is therefore not simply declining production. It is **continuity under load**: keeping sacred machines, industrial chains, expertise, and logistical systems functioning while the demand placed on them can exceed the rate at which they can be restored.

Three recurring forms of deterioration shape this history:

- **depletion** — stocks, feedstocks, specialist materials, components, and other consumable depth are used faster than equivalent replacements can be obtained;
- **breakdown** — machines, infrastructure, transports, and manufactoria accumulate damage faster than ideal maintenance can reverse it;
- **context loss** — irreplaceable expertise, records, calibration knowledge, ritual understanding, and the practical knowledge connecting one system to another disappear when personnel and institutions are lost.

These processes reinforce one another. A machine can remain physically present while the knowledge or supply chain required to use it disappears; conversely, knowledge can survive while the material system it describes becomes impossible to maintain.

## Relationship with Sacratus

The Mechanicus is one of the principal jurisdictions of Sacratus and holds a seat in the five-part **Consistorium**.

It is not an administrative department of the Palatinae. Its authority, sacred obligations, and industrial responsibilities remain distinct under the **Concordat of Sacratus**.

The Long Vigil nevertheless creates deep interdependence. The Forge World needs military protection, food, transport, labor, and strategic prioritization; every other major Sacratan institution depends in turn on Mechanicus production, repair, power, technical personnel, and infrastructure.

That interdependence is productive during the Reformation and High Vigil but becomes politically dangerous during the Low Vigil, when defense authority is used to force choices the Mechanicus regards as intrusions into its own sacred and institutional domain.

## High Vigil and Great Ravenna

The Reformation eventually restores enough stability for Sacratan industry to recover rather than merely consume inherited capacity.

During the High Vigil, **Archmagos Teren-44** becomes one of the most important established Mechanicus figures. He independently reaches the same broad strategic conclusion as Cassian regarding Great Ravenna: genuine agricultural surplus can create positive feedback across population, labor, logistics, and industry rather than merely relieving an immediate food shortage.

The Great Ravenna project works for generations. Reduced subsistence pressure frees industrial capacity for maintenance, defense, expansion, and long-term investment.

Its destruction in **703.M40** therefore removes not only food production but a major engine of Sacratan recovery.

## The Grand Calculus

Under Constantine V Leontios, the Mechanicus becomes one of the central targets of the system-wide **Contraction**.

The formal Mechanicus process is known as:

> **The Grand Calculus of Necessary Diminution, Translation, and Continuance of the Sacred Manufactoria of Sacratus**

The Grand Calculus identifies industrial capacity that can no longer be sustainably maintained and concentrates surviving resources into a smaller, supportable core.

The process is not a voluntary exercise performed by an entirely willing priesthood. Leontios uses the Concordat, defense allocation, and the broad reach of Ultimum to force an accounting of capacity and obligations. Mechanicus critics regard the result as coercive even where the formal authority of the priesthood is preserved.

The central objective is not to maximize the number of manufactoria that remain nominally open. It is to preserve **useful sustainable service**.

Exact aggregate losses remain partly **PROVISIONAL**, but the established scale is severe: roughly sixty percent of the maintained industrial footprint is lost or abandoned, while nominal capacity falls toward roughly one third of its former level. The decline in genuinely usable sustainable output is smaller than those headline numbers because the Calculus deliberately removes capacity that could no longer be maintained honestly.

See [`industry.md`](industry.md) for the industrial logic and [`history.md`](history.md) for its place in the wider Long Vigil.

## Form and service

A recurring Long-Vigil Mechanicus tension can be summarized as **form versus service**.

If a sacred manufactorium can no longer be kept intact, does continuity require preserving that specific installation in recognizable form for as long as possible, or can its components be translated into other systems so that its function survives elsewhere?

The setting does not currently establish this as two neat formal sects. It is a recurring technical, institutional, and religious problem produced by scarcity.

Sacratan Mechanicus practice increasingly accepts reclamation, translation, cannibalization, and concentration when those actions preserve service that would otherwise disappear. That pragmatism does not make the priesthood secular or indifferent to sacred form; it makes the definition of faithful preservation contested under conditions where not everything can survive.

## Post-Leontian recoil

The Grand Calculus leaves lasting institutional damage.

Under Andronikos, the Mechanicus becomes more protective of information, reserves, and internal discretion. The problem is not disloyalty. It is that the Reformation taught institutions to depend on one another, while the Contraction demonstrated that disclosed dependencies can become leverage.

Mechanicus compartmentalization becomes one of the major fault lines of the **Long Tension**.

## Reintegration

When the Warp storm collapses in **999.M41**, the Sacratan Mechanicus encounters Mars and the wider Adeptus Mechanicus after almost nineteen centuries of isolation.

The local priesthood regards itself as a continuous loyal Mechanicus institution. Outside authorities nevertheless have obvious reasons to verify records, technical lineages, doctrinal continuity, industrial claims, and practices that evolved without contact with Mars.

The resulting process of **verification and contest** is established as an important Reintegration problem; its exact institutions, accusations, compromises, and outcomes remain **OPEN**.

Reintegration therefore presents the same inversion seen elsewhere in Sacratus: the Mechanicus spent the Long Vigil learning how to preserve service without external depth, and must now determine how those adaptations fit within a restored hierarchy that can once again supply, inspect, command, and dispute it.
