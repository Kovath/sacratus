# Sacratan Mechanicus — Overview

> **Canon note:** This file is the high-level canonical reference for the Mechanicus of Sacratus. Unmarked statements are established canon. Material explicitly marked **PROVISIONAL**, **OPEN**, or **UNKNOWN IN-UNIVERSE** retains that status.

## Identity

**Sacratus is a Forge World** and the industrial heart of the Sacratus system.

During the Great Crusade and Horus Heresy era, Sacratus is already an important **Mechanicum logistics and industrial nexus**. Its strategic role is especially concentrated in the immense web of intermediary production that keeps Imperial campaigns functioning: ammunition, tank treads, replacement assemblies, essential components, standardized parts, and countless other inputs that are shipped onward to front-line forces and other production centers.

Sacratus can also produce finished war materiel, including complete vehicles such as Leman Russ tanks, but this is not the primary reason the Forge World matters to the wider Imperial logistics network. Its deeper value is that many different production lines elsewhere can depend on Sacratan components, stores, repair capacity, and throughput.

That specialization makes pre-Breaking Sacratus powerful but deliberately non-autarkic. Large volumes of raw material, specialist components, replacement expertise, and other inputs can arrive through the wider Imperium because the Forge World is designed as part of a much larger industrial system rather than as a closed economy.

After the Heresy, the local priesthood continues as part of the **Adeptus Mechanicus**. This directory uses *Mechanicus* for the faction across the setting while retaining *Mechanicum* where historically appropriate to the pre-Adeptus era.

The Sacratan Mechanicus remains loyal to Mars and the Imperium throughout the Long Vigil. Isolation does not turn it into a formally independent or separatist machine cult. The defining problem is instead how a Forge World built around specialization, imported depth, and system-wide logistics survives for almost nineteen centuries when that wider network becomes unreachable.

## From nexus to closed industrial system

The Breaking does two things at once.

It removes the external flows on which Sacratus was built, and it creates a war that asks the Forge World to produce **the same or more** with less material depth, less replacement capacity, and no reliable outside relief.

Raw materials, specialist components, replacement personnel, external knowledge, shipping, and industrial redundancy beyond the system disappear behind the storm. At the same time, ammunition expenditure, repairs, fortification, replacement equipment, transport, and emergency infrastructure all become more urgent.

From that point onward, Sacratan industry must increasingly reproduce, repair, substitute, reclaim, and prioritize from within the resources still reachable inside the system.

The central Mechanicus problem of the Long Vigil is therefore not simply declining production. It is **continuity under load**: keeping sacred machines, industrial chains, expertise, and logistical systems functioning while demands remain high enough to consume the very capacity required to sustain future production.

## The three Mechanicus crises

The Mechanicus later understands its Long-Vigil industrial history through three major named crises. These are not merely categories of industrial weakness; they are remembered as distinct historical crises whose sequence matters, even though their exact dates and boundaries remain **OPEN**.

1. **The Depletion** — the crisis of material depth. Imported stocks, specialist inputs, consumables, and other externally supplied resources are drawn down faster than equivalent local sources can replace them.
2. **The Breakdown** — formally **Cascading Manufactorum Failure** — the crisis of production itself. Accumulated overuse, deferred maintenance, missing inputs, and interdependent failures turn isolated breakdowns into cascading loss across linked manufactoria. **Teren-44**, then a comparatively mid-ranking Magos Logis, is the first established figure to recognize that these apparently separate failures form one system-wide crisis.
3. **The Context Loss** — formally **Operational Knowledge Continuity Failure** — the crisis of understanding. Personnel, records, calibration knowledge, production context, and practical institutional memory disappear far enough that physically surviving machinery or instructions can no longer guarantee usable capability.

The sequence is therefore **materials → production → context**.

The underlying processes do not disappear when a named crisis ends. Depletion, breakdown, and context loss remain recurring pressures for the rest of the Long Vigil, but the capitalized names refer to historical moments when each problem becomes impossible for the Mechanicus to treat as a local or temporary failure.

See [`history.md`](history.md), [`timeline.md`](timeline.md), and [`industry.md`](industry.md) for the historical and industrial treatment of these crises.

## Relationship with Sacratus

The Mechanicus is one of the principal jurisdictions of Sacratus and holds a seat in the five-part **Consistorium**.

It is not an administrative department of the Palatinae. Its authority, sacred obligations, and industrial responsibilities remain distinct under the **Concordat of Sacratus**.

The Long Vigil nevertheless creates deep interdependence. The Forge World needs military protection, food, transport, labor, and strategic prioritization; every other major Sacratan institution depends in turn on Mechanicus production, repair, power, technical personnel, and infrastructure.

That interdependence is productive during the Reformation and High Vigil but becomes politically dangerous during the Low Vigil, when defense authority is used to force choices the Mechanicus regards as intrusions into its own sacred and institutional domain.

## Teren-44 and Great Ravenna

Teren-44's historical importance begins with **The Breakdown**, not with Great Ravenna.

His Breakdown-era Logis work exposes hidden dependencies across industrial domains and makes a system-wide failure legible where local reporting had treated it as a collection of separate problems. That diagnosis establishes the reputation and analytic framework he later carries into the mature High Vigil.

During Constantine III Cassian's reign, Teren's experience of interdependence helps him recognize the wider strategic significance of surplus. His analysis converges with Cassian's conclusion that genuine agricultural abundance can create positive feedback across population, labor, logistics, and industry rather than merely relieve an immediate food shortage.

Teren therefore becomes associated with Great Ravenna as the project develops, but he is **not established as its originator**, and Great Ravenna should not replace The Breakdown as his defining early contribution.

Great Ravenna is also a major **Mechanicus investment**, not merely an agricultural project from which the Forge World passively benefits. Over its century-scale development, the Mechanicus commits substantial industrial plant, tooling, stores, technical manpower, and senior expertise to Ravenna.

The project works for generations. Reduced subsistence pressure frees industrial capacity for maintenance, defense, expansion, and long-term investment, while Ravenna itself becomes a concentration of Mechanicus capital and expertise.

Its destruction in **703.M40** therefore inflicts a double loss: Sacratus loses a major engine of abundance, and the Mechanicus loses a significant body of machinery, materiel, manpower, and expertise that had been deliberately concentrated there.

Teren-44 rises to **Archmagos Logis only sometime after Ravenna**. The exact date and circumstances of that promotion remain **OPEN**.

## The Grand Calculus

Under Constantine V Leontios, the Mechanicus becomes one of the central targets of the system-wide **Contraction**.

The formal Mechanicus process is known as:

> **The Grand Calculus of Necessary Diminution, Translation, and Continuance of the Sacred Manufactoria of Sacratus**

The Grand Calculus identifies industrial capacity that can no longer be sustainably maintained and concentrates surviving resources into a smaller, supportable core.

The process is not a voluntary exercise performed by an entirely willing priesthood. Leontios uses the Concordat, defense allocation, and the broad reach of Ultimum to force an accounting of capacity and obligations. Mechanicus critics regard the result as coercive even where the formal authority of the priesthood is preserved.

The central objective is not to maximize the number of manufactoria that remain nominally open. It is to preserve **useful sustainable service**.

Exact aggregate losses remain partly **PROVISIONAL**, but the established scale is severe: roughly sixty percent of the maintained industrial footprint is lost or abandoned, while nominal capacity falls toward roughly one third of its former level. The decline in genuinely usable sustainable output is smaller than those headline numbers because the Calculus deliberately removes capacity that could no longer be maintained honestly.

See [`industry.md`](industry.md) for the industrial logic and [`history.md`](history.md) for its place in the wider Long Vigil.

## Form and service

A recurring Long-Vigil Mechanicus tension can be summarized as **form versus service**.

If a sacred manufactorium can no longer be kept intact, does continuity require preserving that specific installation in recognizable form for as long as possible, or can its components be translated into other systems so that its function survives elsewhere?

The setting does not currently establish this as two neat formal sects. It is a recurring technical, institutional, and religious problem produced by scarcity.

Sacratan Mechanicus practice increasingly accepts reclamation, translation, cannibalization, and concentration when those actions preserve service that would otherwise disappear. That pragmatism does not make the priesthood secular or indifferent to sacred form; it makes the definition of faithful preservation contested under conditions where not everything can survive.

## Post-Leontian recoil

The Grand Calculus leaves lasting institutional damage.

Under Andronikos, the Mechanicus becomes more protective of information, reserves, and internal discretion. The problem is not disloyalty. It is that the Reformation taught institutions to depend on one another, while the Contraction demonstrated that disclosed dependencies can become leverage.

Mechanicus compartmentalization becomes one of the major fault lines of the **Long Tension**.

## Reintegration

When the Warp storm collapses in **999.M41**, the Sacratan Mechanicus encounters Mars and the wider Adeptus Mechanicus after almost nineteen centuries of isolation.

The local priesthood regards itself as a continuous loyal Mechanicus institution. Outside authorities nevertheless have obvious reasons to verify records, technical lineages, doctrinal continuity, industrial claims, and practices that evolved without contact with Mars.

The resulting process of **verification and contest** is established as an important Reintegration problem; its exact institutions, accusations, compromises, and outcomes remain **OPEN**.

Reintegration therefore presents the same inversion seen elsewhere in Sacratus: the Mechanicus spent the Long Vigil learning how to preserve service without external depth, and must now determine how those adaptations fit within a restored hierarchy that can once again supply, inspect, command, and dispute it.
