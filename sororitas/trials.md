# The Trials of the Sacratan Sororitas

> **Purpose:** Canonical home for the Sororitas tradition of the **Trials**: a retrospective religious periodization of crises that tested Sacratus's faith, endurance, discernment, unity, truth, and identity. The Trials are not simply a list of the largest battles, and the virtue labels sometimes useful to writers are not an official in-universe taxonomy.

## The tradition

The dominant Sacratan Sororitas tradition recognizes **seven principal Trials**. They are not treated as seven cleanly bounded military campaigns. Some are wars, some are social or political catastrophes, and some are theological crises created by the way Sacratans interpreted the Emperor's continued **Light**.

The numbering is a later religious and historical framework rather than proof that every contemporary understood an event as a Trial while living through it. Convents, theologians, and historians can dispute exact boundaries, subordinate crises, names, and whether some events should be divided or combined. The sevenfold sequence below is nevertheless the dominant tradition.

The Trials are central to the development of the mature Sororitas conviction:

> **He has not abandoned us.**

That phrase does not mean rescue is promised. Across the Trials, easier interpretations of the Light are repeatedly broken down until continued divine relationship comes to imply continued obligation rather than guaranteed deliverance.

## I — The First Trial / The Breaking

The **Breaking** in **100.M40** is the first and defining Trial.

The surrounding heavens vanish or distort behind the Warp phenomenon. Sacratus is isolated, mass Chaos assault begins, Augusta is attacked, and the system's ordinary relationship with the wider Imperium collapses.

Yet the Astronomican remains visible as the distant **Light**.

For the Sisters, this becomes the defining religious fact of the catastrophe:

> **When all other lights were extinguished, His remained.**

Early interpretations naturally bind the continuing Light to hope that the Emperor has not abandoned Sacratus and that relief may still come. Later Trials force the Sororitas to separate those two ideas. The Light can remain even when rescue does not.

The First Trial therefore remains the event against which later Trials are measured.

## II — The Years of Ash

The **Years of Ash** belong to the early Reformation after the immediate catastrophe of the Breaking.

Surviving the first invasion does not mean Sacratus has become sustainable. External trade and reinforcement are gone. Agricultural and industrial systems are damaged or disrupted. Refugees concentrate into surviving regions. The wider system simultaneously faces the material pressures remembered elsewhere as the early Depletion and Breakdown.

Food scarcity becomes one of the defining human experiences of the period. The exact combination and chronology of harvest failures, lost imports, damaged agricultural capacity, transport failures, and other causes remain **OPEN** rather than being reduced to a single Warp explanation.

Under **Canoness Theresa**, Sororitas institutions become particularly important in relief work: hospitals, refugee care, food distribution, and the preservation of social continuity alongside their military responsibilities.

A traditional formulation of the Trial is:

> **The body hungered. The soul did not.**

The theological consequence is severe. The Light still shines, but the faithful still starve. Its presence therefore cannot be treated as a guarantee that physical relief will arrive before suffering does.

Some later traditions treat the Years of Ash as several lesser crises rather than one continuous Trial; the dominant sequence groups them together.

## III — The War of the False Light

During the later Reformation or early High Vigil, another luminous phenomenon appears in the heavens in apparent proximity to the Emperor's Light.

It appears nearer and more accessible than the true Astronomican. Dreams, visions, and religious claims gather around it. Movements arise around the belief that the Emperor has finally provided a path out of Sacratus.

Navigator and other specialist observations reject the phenomenon as the true Astronomican or a genuine route to Terra. Ships that attempt to follow it disappear or fail to return.

The exact nature of the **False Light** is **UNKNOWN IN-UNIVERSE**. Later accounts describe it variously as a daemonic deception, a Warp entity, an emergent phenomenon of the surrounding storm, or something never satisfactorily classified. The scale of clerical, civilian, and military adherence is disputed, as is whether any Sisters themselves fell to it.

What makes the crisis especially dangerous is that the deception does not ask Sacratans to reject the Emperor. It claims to speak in His name and to offer the deliverance for which many have prayed.

The Sororitas lesson becomes:

> **Not every light is His Light.**

The War of the False Light becomes an important historical root of Sacratan Sororitas suspicion toward uncontrolled prophets, unauthorized miracle cults, and visions that demand obedience merely because they present themselves as divine revelation.

## IV — The Empty Thrones

The **Empty Thrones** occurs during **Justinian's** reign.

A cluster of deaths and vacancies among senior Sacratan authorities destabilizes relationships that had become heavily dependent on particular officeholders during the Reformation. The exact identities, causes, and sequence of those losses remain **OPEN**.

Successors inherit unresolved disputes over resources, jurisdiction, and the continued reach of **Ultimum**. Contradictory orders circulate. Armed institutions mobilize. For a time, Sacratus comes close to fighting itself.

No general civil war begins.

The eventual settlement restores the **Consistorium** as the arena in which the crisis must be resolved and contributes to precedents intended to keep Ultimum from becoming simple permanent Astartes rule. Justinian participates in that settlement, but the Trial is not remembered as a crisis solved merely because a Constantine returned and commanded obedience. Its importance lies in the institutions being forced back into a process they can continue to recognize as legitimate.

The exact legal precedents, personalities, and sequence of mobilizations remain **OPEN**.

The Empty Thrones is distinct from the much later **Concordat Crisis / Long Tension**. The former is an acute succession and authority crisis within the still-forming Reformation settlement. The latter grows from centuries of accumulated distrust after emergency cooperation has repeatedly proven capable of becoming coercion.

For the Sororitas, the enemy of the Fourth Trial is not an external invader. It is the possibility that faithful Sacratan institutions can destroy Sacratus themselves.

## V — The Silent War

The **Silent War** belongs to the High Vigil before Ravenna.

Its surviving records describe a crisis in which the reliability of information itself becomes suspect. People disappear. Orders appear without clear origin. Units are redirected toward objectives that do not exist when they arrive. Archives no longer agree with one another. Astropathic and administrative messages appear to contain impossible provenance. Witnesses remember meetings that other participants deny occurred.

The Sisters respond in part by maintaining independent handwritten and physically witnessed records alongside compromised official systems. These records are not treated as magically immune to corruption; their value is that they create separate chains of evidence that can be compared against one another. Other Sacratan institutions likewise adopt increasingly physical verification of orders and identity.

The cause and even unity of the Silent War are **UNKNOWN IN-UNIVERSE**.

Some histories describe a coordinated Chaos infiltration campaign, often with Tzeentchian associations. Others argue that several unrelated incidents of infiltration, sabotage, Warp disturbance, and institutional paranoia were later combined into a single religious narrative. Nobody can securely establish when the Silent War began or when it ended.

Its importance to Sororitas memory is precisely that uncertainty. Faith cannot depend on assuming that every apparent certainty, record, memory, or command is trustworthy simply because it arrives in an authoritative form.

## VI — The Trial of the Empty Table / Ravenna

The **Exterminatus of Ravenna** in **703.M40** becomes the center of the Sixth Trial, commonly called the **Trial of the Empty Table**.

The Sororitas memory is deliberately broader than the moment of Exterminatus.

They remember Ravenna's genuine prosperity and the lives improved by it. They remember the corruption crisis, evacuation triage, those who could not be saved, the destruction of the world, the loss of billions of loyal people, and the food scarcity, displacement, and institutional shock that follow.

For the Sisters, the Trial therefore does not end when Ravenna burns. Its boundaries extend into the human aftermath, though theologians disagree over exactly where the Sixth Trial should be considered complete.

The Sororitas do not need to conclude that Cassian's Exterminatus was unnecessary in order to mourn those it condemned. The central Low-Vigil lesson becomes:

> **Necessity does not absolve suffering.**

A decision can be required for survival and still leave obligations toward the dead, the displaced, and those who had no acceptable choice.

This becomes an important theological foundation for the Sisters' later role during **Leontios's Contraction**. The institution may accept that a settlement cannot be saved while still insisting that the people abandoned with it must not disappear into the arithmetic of necessity.

The exact rites, memorial practices, symbols, and duration associated with the Trial of the Empty Table remain **OPEN**.

## VII — The Return

The **Seventh Trial** begins when the Long Vigil ends in **999.M41**.

The stars return. The wider Imperium returns. Sacratus is no longer alone.

For the Sororitas, this is not merely the end of the testing begun at the Breaking. It creates a new kind of test.

For almost nineteen centuries, the local Sisters preserved Imperial faith without dependable contact with the institutions that normally define, supervise, and reinforce it. Reintegration brings outside Ecclesiastical authorities, off-world Sororitas, Imperial investigators, and new judgments about practices that Sacratans have inherited across dozens of generations.

The Sacratan Sororitas regard themselves as loyal and continuous. Outside authorities must nevertheless determine what changed, what remains acceptable, and how local traditions fit within wider Imperial religious structures. The exact degree of criticism, accommodation, suspicion, or acceptance remains **OPEN** and should not be presumed uniformly hostile.

The Seventh Trial is therefore ongoing.

Its central problem is no longer whether the Sisters can remain Imperial without the Imperium physically present. It is whether nearly nineteen centuries of survival can be reconciled with the Imperium now physically present again.

No canonical end to the Seventh Trial has been established.

## Theological development

The Trials should not be reduced in-universe to a tidy list in which each event exists to teach one virtue. Their historical consequences overlap, their boundaries are disputed, and later theologians often disagree about what exactly was learned.

Taken together, however, they explain the mature Sacratan Sororitas interpretation of the Light.

The First Trial establishes that the Emperor's Light remains. The Years of Ash demonstrate that remaining does not guarantee relief. The False Light makes apparent deliverance itself suspect. The Empty Thrones demonstrates that loyal institutions can become a threat to one another. The Silent War attacks confidence in testimony and authority. Ravenna demonstrates that necessity can be real without erasing suffering. The Return forces the Sisters to test the identity they built in isolation against the wider Imperium they spent the Vigil serving.

The surviving conclusion is not that the Emperor guarantees safety.

It is that the relationship has not ended, and therefore neither has the obligation.
